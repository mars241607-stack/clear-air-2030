Part 1 Analysis

Target Audience & Youth Risk Profiles

Focus: Australian Youth & Adolescents (12–17 Years)

Adolescent Smoking & Vaping Prevalence (ASSAD Data)

Australian Secondary Students' Alcohol and Drug Survey Trends

12–17 Yrs

Gender Disparity (Female Youth Risk)

Recent ASSAD data indicates 35% of female secondary students have ever vaped compared to 25% of male students. In the past month, 19% of females vaped vs 13% of males. Targeted campaigns must address stress, mood management, and aesthetic marketing directed at young women.

Adolescent Transition Window (12–15 Yrs)

While older teens (16–17) show higher cumulative rates (43% ever vaped), early adolescence (ages 12–15) is the critical initiation window, where 24% have tried vapes. Intervening early prevents lifetime nicotine dependency before habits solidify.

Key Target Demographics

  • Primary Target: Secondary School Students & Adolescents (Ages 12–17) undergoing critical social transitions and peer influences.
  • High-Risk Subgroup: Female secondary students showing higher current monthly vaping rates (19%) than males (13%).
  • Vulnerable Population Groups: Regional/rural adolescents and First Nations youth who experience higher commercial tobacco exposure and healthcare access barriers.
  • Supply Pathway: 60% of young people obtain vapes through peers or social networks, making peer dynamics the central focus of intervention.
AUDIENCE INSIGHT SUMMARY

Scare tactics and guilt trips don't work on teens. Young people know cigarettes are harmful, but disposable vapes were packaged to look like harmless, fun accessories. Our approach treats teens like smart adults: exposing corporate tactics, keeping things real, and helping them back their own choices.

Part 2 Strategy

Channel Selection & Youth Reach

Multi-Channel Exposure Model

1. Social Media (TikTok & Instagram Reels)

Teenagers spend an average of 2.5 hours daily on short-form video feeds. The campaign utilizes 15-30 second short video formats featuring youth creators, humor, myth-busting, and "behind the scenes" chemical breakdowns.

Key Tactic:
Algorithmic retargeting ensuring 5+ impressions per week per user.

2. Schools, Youth Hubs & Sports Clubs

High-impact posters and interactive posters placed near school campuses, community centers, sports facilities, and transit stops. QR codes link directly to interactive quizzes and support tools.

Key Tactic:
QR stickers on water fountains and recreation centers: "Refresh with Clean Air".

3. Peer Leaders & Mentorship

Peer-led discussion groups integrated into school wellbeing programs and youth community centers. Youth mentors facilitate real-world refusal skills roleplay, dismantling the myth that "everyone is doing it".

Key Tactic:
Interactive decision app ("Do I or Don't I?") completed in groups.

Part 3 Evidence

Targeted Risk Behaviours & Evidence

Evidence-Based Prevention

Core Risk Behaviours to Change

1. The "Sweet Flavors" Hook 74% Teens Prefer Flavors

Mango, watermelon, blue razz—flavors are engineered to make chemical aerosol go down smoothly. Over 74% of teens who vape start because of fruity options. We show how artificial flavorings cover up heavy nicotine doses and chemical harshness.

2. Social Vaping & Group Pressure The Group Loop

Passing a vape around in a bathroom stall or at a weekend hangout often feels like a quick way to bond. We equip young people with effortless, natural ways to say no without making things awkward or feeling left out.

3. The "Vaping Relieves Stress" Myth The Anxiety Trap

Many teens reach for a vape when exam week or social life gets intense, believing it calms them down. In reality, nicotine spikes your heart rate and withdrawal between puffs actually *creates* the anxiety. Breaking this cycle brings genuine peace of mind.

Part 4 Evaluation

Evaluation & National Tobacco Strategy 2023–2030 Impact

Predicted Success Analysis

Clarity of Audience & Message

High Potential: By tailoring tone directly to 12–17 year olds without patronizing language, message clarity is maximized. Focus on chemical transparency rather than generic fear tactics ensures higher resonance.

Healthcare System Relief

Preventing youth nicotine dependency reduces future incidence of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), cardiovascular disease, and asthma, saving the Australian healthcare system billions in hospital admissions.

Life Expectancy & Wellbeing

Nicotine addiction during adolescence permanently alters prefrontal cortex brain development. Early prevention yields substantial gains in Quality-Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) and adolescent mental health stability.

2030 National 5% Target

Combining Australian pharmacy-only prescription legislation with youth campaigns provides the dual regulatory and cultural push required to bring daily youth smoking/vaping under 5% by 2030.

Overall Campaign Evaluation Statement

The CLEAR AIR 2030 strategy directly aligns with Priority 1 & 4 of the National Tobacco Strategy 2023–2030 by dismantling the tobacco and vaping industry's youth pipeline. By combining non-preachy social media storytelling, physical youth touchpoints, and peer leadership, the campaign achieves high visibility and multiple exposures. The evidence confirms that reducing adolescent vaping prevents future transition into combustible tobacco (where vapers are 3x more likely to smoke cigarettes later in life). Consequently, this campaign is evaluated as highly effective, offering high prevention success that directly protects young lives and alleviates strain on Australia's public health infrastructure.
